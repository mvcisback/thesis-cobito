Concepts and their Representations

Knight: The name of the song is called 'Haddocks' Eyes'!

Alice: Oh, that's the name of the song, is it?

Knight: No, you don't understand, that's what the name is called.

This thesis lies at the intersection of many sub-disciplines, and as the chapter quote suggests, these chapters develop a consistent terminology and mathematical machinery. We begin with a brief introduction to formal languages, concept classes, and representation classes, laying the groundwork for encoding the set of behaviors that constitute an agent completing a task. We start with the abstract formalism of a concept class.

Formal languages and automata

We will later care about which sequences of states and observations constitute completing a task. A sequence is formalized as a string.

String

Let Σ\alphabetΣ be a set of symbols, an alphabet. A string is a finite sequence of symbols from Σ\alphabetΣ; ∣x∣|x|∣x∣ is its length. The empty string is the unique string of length 000, written ϵ\emptystringϵ. The concatenation of strings x,yx, yx,y is x⋅yx \concat yx⋅y, with xxx the prefix and yyy the suffix. For a natural number kkk, xkx^kxk is xxx concatenated with itself kkk times, where x0=defϵx^0 \eqdef \emptystringx0=defϵ.
